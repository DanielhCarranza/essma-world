# Product Requirements Document — Essma World

## Product

**Essma World** is a cozy, single-player 2D web game where a child explores Essma de María’s ranch in Sonora, dresses Essma and her animal friends, and gradually opens gentle adventures. The feeling is warm, creative, nature-connected, and distinctly Sonoran.

### Audience

- Primary player: one Spanish-speaking child, 5–10 years old.
- Secondary audience: parent/caregiver who may help with setup and backups.
- Platform: touch-first mobile web and desktop browser through Sites.

### Product principles

- Start with delight and agency: no failure state, timer pressure, or grind in dress-up.
- Use clear visuals and short Mexican-Spanish copy for independent play.
- Celebrate Sonoran landscapes, animals, and traditions with respectful review.
- Protect children: no advertising, purchases, accounts, chat, outbound sharing, tracking, or behavioral profiling.

## Story and cast

Essma finds an old map in her family’s ranch barn. Each restored ranch space and later region reveals a friendly clue about Sonora and the friends who live there.

NPCs and future companions are flexible until approved through the content pipeline.

## Core loop

1. Enter the ranch and choose a friend or activity.
2. Dress Essma or a launch companion.
3. See the choice reflected immediately in the ranch scene.
4. Complete a gentle activity or collect a reward in later phases.
5. Unlock new looks, décor, story clues, and places.

## Release scope

### Playable Core (first build)

- Main menu and Mexican-Spanish settings.
- One Ranch de Essma 2D scene.
- Dress-up for Essma, Juancito, Tori, and Anita.
- Ten starter items across supported layers.
- Immediate preview, equip/unequip, randomize, and reset actions.
- Local single-player save, audio/reduced-motion controls, and parent backup/restore entry point.

### Explicitly deferred

- Decoration placement, economy, quests, region map, shop, photo mode, festivals, multiplayer, cloud profiles, payments, ads, and 3D gameplay.
- Educational objectives in the initial dress-up activity.

## Success criteria

- A child can independently open dress-up, change a look, and return to the ranch on touch or desktop.
- Equipped looks and settings survive a normal revisit on the same device.
- The experience remains useful with sound off and reduced motion enabled.
- No child data leaves the device in the first release.
